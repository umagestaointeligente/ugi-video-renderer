# UGI Benchmark — Fernando Miranda Storytelling V1

Date: 2026-09-13
Status: ACTIVE_REFERENCE
Scope: Histórias de Gestão / company-history storytelling for Instagram Reels, TikTok, LinkedIn video and YouTube longform.

## What to emulate

Emulate the functional storytelling grammar, not exact scripts, wording, typography, colors, branding or shot-for-shot composition.

### 1. Hook first
- First 1–3 seconds must contain a curiosity gap, contradiction, surprising business fact or counterintuitive claim.
- Avoid greetings, logo-only intro or setup before the hook.
- Strong patterns: “A empresa nunca vendeu apenas X”, “O detalhe que mudou Y”, “Como uma decisão improvável criou Z”, “O que parece simples esconde um negócio enorme”.

### 2. Immediate visual proof
- Brand/company/product/person must appear immediately when named.
- Use real historical or current visuals whenever rights/provenance permit.
- Presenter may appear simultaneously with the proof visual, but never at the expense of legibility.

### 3. Story arc for short-form
Recommended 45–75s structure:
1. Hook / curiosity gap — 0–3s
2. Context — 3–10s
3. Origin/problem — 10–20s
4. Turning point / decisive choice — 20–35s
5. Business mechanism — 35–50s
6. Payoff / surprising consequence — 50–65s
7. Management lesson / close — final 5–10s

### 4. Story arc for extended Reels / LinkedIn
Recommended 75–120s:
- Hook
- Historical context
- Problem/tension
- Decision
- How the model actually worked
- Scale/result
- What managers should learn
- One-line close/CTA

### 5. Story arc for YouTube longform
Recommended 8–15 min:
- Cold open built from the strongest payoff
- Origin story
- Founder/company context
- First inflection point
- Distribution/product/capital/operations mechanism
- Second inflection point
- Scale
- Mistakes/constraints when relevant
- Three management lessons
- Close that connects the past to a current strategic question

## Visual grammar observed in the user-provided screenshot

Visible reel covers repeatedly combine:
- recognizable company/product/history visual in the upper part;
- the presenter speaking to camera in a lower panel;
- a short, high-contrast headline promising a story;
- one dominant idea per cover.

Examples visible in the screenshot include stories about Dove, Yakult, Coca-Cola, a luxury-bag rental model and Hospital Sírio-Libanês. These examples suggest the recurring editorial product is “business/history story with a surprising angle”, rather than generic business tips.

## UGI adaptation

UGI should use a two-layer composition when it improves comprehension:
- upper/main zone: real company/product/founder/history visual;
- lower/presenter or branded explanation zone: narration support;
- headline inside vertical safe area;
- closed captions only in the dedicated lower caption band, max 2 lines, chunked and synchronized;
- no important text within crop-prone side margins or Instagram UI zones.

UGI Safe Area V2 remains mandatory. The benchmark must never override platform-safe layout rules.

## Editorial formula

For company stories use:

SURPRISE → ORIGIN → CONFLICT → DECISION → MECHANISM → CONSEQUENCE → MANAGEMENT LESSON

Every story must answer:
- What is surprising here?
- What was the original problem?
- What decision changed the trajectory?
- How did the business mechanism actually work?
- What measurable or visible consequence followed?
- What should a manager/entrepreneur take from it?

## Hook library — functional patterns only

- “Essa empresa ficou gigante por causa de uma decisão que parecia pequena.”
- “O produto que você conhece não era o verdadeiro negócio.”
- “A virada começou quando a empresa parou de fazer o óbvio.”
- “Todo mundo viu o produto. Pouca gente percebeu o modelo de negócio.”
- “O erro parecia operacional. Na verdade, era estratégico.”
- “A empresa cresceu quando decidiu fazer menos — mas melhor.”

Do not reuse Fernando Miranda’s exact phrasing from specific videos.

## Platform adaptation

### Instagram Reels
- 45–90s preferred depending on story density.
- First frame must explain the curiosity gap without audio.
- Presenter + proof visual can coexist.
- Strong payoff before the final CTA.

### TikTok
- Faster first 2 seconds.
- Less institutional framing.
- More tension, contradiction and consequence.
- Same factual rigor and visual proof.

### LinkedIn
- More executive interpretation.
- Reduce sensational phrasing.
- Emphasize decision, trade-off, governance, capital, margin, operations or leadership.

### YouTube
- Expand the same story into chapters instead of stretching the short-form script.
- Add factual sourcing, chronology, competing explanations and management lessons.

## Hard gates

BLOCK publication if any of these fail:
- Hook is generic or delayed.
- Named company/person lacks real/contextual visual proof when obtainable.
- Visuals do not match narration.
- Text/captions cover important visual information.
- Closed caption exceeds 2 lines or leaves the dedicated caption band.
- Story lacks a clear turning point or management takeaway.
- Factual source cannot support the main claim.
- Rights/provenance is unclear.
- Script copies another creator’s exact wording or structure too literally.

## Success metrics

Short-form primary:
- 3-second view rate / hold
- average watch time
- retention percentage
- completion rate where available
- shares
- saves

Secondary:
- comments
- follows generated
- profile visits
- replays/reposts where available

Longform primary:
- CTR
- 30-second retention
- average percentage viewed
- average view duration
- returning viewers
- end-screen continuation

## Operational rule

From this version forward, “Histórias de Gestão” scripts must be evaluated against this benchmark before render and again after render QA. The objective is to reproduce the high-retention storytelling mechanics while keeping UGI’s own visual identity, factual standards and platform-safe layout.
