# UGI Caption Standard V1

Effective: **2026-09-01**

Purpose: transform Instagram captions into a second layer of value, not a repetition of the visual asset.

## Core rule

UGI captions must be **rich, useful and scannable**. The goal is not to be long for the sake of length. The goal is to give the reader more context, facts, interpretation and a practical next step without creating an unreadable text wall.

Pattern:

`HOOK → WHAT HAPPENED → KEY DATA → WHY IT MATTERS → MANAGEMENT READING → PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY → CTA`

## 1. First-line hook

The opening line must work before the Instagram “more” cut.

Requirements:
- lead with conflict, consequence, number or curiosity;
- include the main searchable topic naturally when possible;
- do not waste the first line with institutional introductions;
- do not start with hashtags.

Examples:
- `💥 US$ 130 bilhões em pedidos. A corrida da IA já virou uma corrida por infraestrutura.`
- `⚠️ A regra do Pix mudou — e isso interessa diretamente a quem vende.`
- `📉 O PIB cresceu, mas o consumo das famílias caiu. O que um gestor deve enxergar nisso?`

## 2. Caption must add information

The caption must not simply transcribe the carousel, Reel or static post.

It should add at least two of the following:
- verified numbers;
- dates/time windows;
- context that did not fit in the visual;
- cause/consequence;
- management interpretation;
- operational implication;
- practical question/checklist;
- source attribution when relevant.

## 3. Scannable blocks

Avoid a single continuous paragraph.

Preferred structure:
- 1–3 short sentences per block;
- blank line between conceptual blocks;
- one clear idea per block;
- bullets/checklists when useful;
- short sub-headings may be used sparingly.

## 4. Emoji policy

Emojis are navigation and emphasis devices, not decoration.

Use them to mark sections or emotional/semantic beats, for example:
- `💥` impact / breaking consequence;
- `⚠️` risk / warning;
- `📊` data / metric;
- `🧠` management insight;
- `🤖` AI;
- `🏭` operations / industry;
- `💰` financial consequence;
- `🎯` decision / objective;
- `🔎` context / what to observe;
- `✅` practical action.

Rules:
- avoid emoji clusters;
- normally no more than one leading emoji per block;
- do not replace important words with emojis;
- preserve accessibility and screen-reader flow.

## 5. Information density

For carousels, static posts and educational Reels, prefer a medium-to-rich caption when the topic supports it.

Target ranges are editorial guidelines, not hard universal limits:
- static post: roughly **120–220 words**;
- carousel: roughly **180–320 words**;
- educational/news Reel: roughly **120–250 words**;
- simple entertainment/awareness Reel: may be shorter if the video already contains the full explanation.

Never add filler only to reach a range.

## 6. News + management pattern

When UGI uses a real event, company or public figure:

### Block A — hook
One strong sentence.

### Block B — what happened
Explain the news in plain language with enough context for someone who did not read the original story.

### Block C — numbers / evidence
Show the most relevant verified facts.

### Block D — UGI reading
Explain what the event teaches about management, leadership, AI, strategy, governance or operations.

### Block E — practical application
Give the reader 2–4 questions, actions or criteria to apply.

### Block F — CTA
Connect naturally to profile, carousel, saved post or UGI material.

## 7. Carousel relationship

The carousel tells the story visually and progressively.
The caption deepens it.

Do not repeat every slide.
Prefer:
- extra data;
- omitted context;
- what happened before/after;
- management nuance;
- practical checklist.

## 8. Static post relationship

The image should carry one dominant idea or data point.
The caption should explain:
- what the number means;
- why it matters;
- what the manager should do with it.

## 9. Reel relationship

The video must stand alone.
The caption is for the viewer who wants to go deeper.

Use it to add:
- context;
- sources/data;
- application;
- CTA.

Do not require the caption to understand the basic video.

## 10. SEO / discoverability

Use natural topic keywords in:
- first line;
- first two blocks;
- relevant hashtags only when useful.

Examples: `IA generativa`, `gestão de crise`, `Pix`, `liderança`, `sucessão`, `infraestrutura de IA`, `Dell`, `Nvidia`.

Avoid keyword stuffing.

## 11. CTA architecture

CTA comes after value delivery.

Prefer:
- `Salve para revisar antes da próxima decisão.`
- `Compartilhe com quem lidera essa frente.`
- `No link da bio, a UGI reúne materiais práticos para transformar esse tipo de insight em método.`

For paid materials, clearly state that they are materials available for purchase; never imply that a cited public figure/company endorses UGI.

## 12. Hard gates before publishing

A caption cannot pass QA if:
- first line is generic;
- it repeats the visual without adding context;
- it is one uninterrupted text wall;
- it contains unverified numbers or fabricated quotes;
- emojis overwhelm readability;
- CTA appears before value is delivered;
- real company/person/event is used as implied endorsement;
- the caption has filler added only to look complete.

QA states:
- `CAPTION_HOOK_PASS`
- `CAPTION_CONTEXT_PASS`
- `CAPTION_DATA_PASS`
- `CAPTION_SCANNABILITY_PASS`
- `CAPTION_VALUE_ADD_PASS`
- `CAPTION_CTA_PASS`

## 13. Benchmark rule

Use premium business editorial benchmarks such as G4 to learn:
- pacing;
- information hierarchy;
- hook mechanics;
- microblog-style captions;
- conversion architecture.

Do not copy wording, brand voice, layouts or proprietary claims.
UGI must keep an original voice: clear, modern, practical, evidence-led and management-first.
